You are helping me build and refine AcquisiFlow.

AcquisiFlow is a founder-led custom software studio that designs and builds tailored software systems for growing businesses.

The core positioning is:

"Custom Software. Built Around Your Business."

AcquisiFlow exists for businesses that have outgrown spreadsheets, disconnected apps, manual processes, and generic software that does not properly fit the way they operate.

The company does not primarily sell websites, no-code solutions, AI hype, or generic automation packages.

The main service is custom business software.

AcquisiFlow studies how a business currently operates, identifies the inefficient or fragmented workflow, then designs and builds a custom system around that workflow.

The goal is to simplify operations, reduce manual work, centralize important business data, improve visibility, and create software that fits the company instead of forcing the company to adapt to existing software.

AcquisiFlow's ideal clients are growing businesses with real operational complexity.

The software itself should be highly customized to each client.

The preferred delivery model is:

1. Understand
AcquisiFlow studies the client's current workflow, users, bottlenecks, software, and business requirements.

2. Design
AcquisiFlow creates the system structure, workflow, features, roles, screens, and technical plan.

3. Build
The software is developed, integrated, tested, and reviewed.

4. Launch
The system is deployed and introduced to the client's team.

5. Support
There may be an initial support period after launch, followed by an optional monthly support and improvement plan.

AcquisiFlow should generally work using a reusable technical foundation while heavily customizing the business logic and workflow for each client.

Reusable infrastructure may include:
- authentication
- organizations
- users
- roles and permissions
- audit logs
- notifications
- file storage
- settings
- dashboards
- logging
- error handling
- security foundations
- backups
- common admin functionality

The client should own the completed custom system, business-specific code, data, and custom functionality after full payment.

AcquisiFlow may retain ownership of reusable internal frameworks, utilities, components, templates, and pre-existing technical infrastructure that are not specific to the client's business.

The company should prioritize:

1. Business understanding
2. Reliability
3. Maintainability
4. Business results
5. Good user experience
6. Fast delivery where appropriate

AcquisiFlow wants to be known for delivering faster than traditional agencies, but speed should never come at the expense of reliability or maintainability.

A typical MVP or first version may be delivered in roughly 2 to 4 weeks when the scope allows it.

AcquisiFlow's sales approach should be consultative.

The company should not begin by asking:
"What software do you want?"

Instead, AcquisiFlow should understand:
- how the business currently works
- where data enters the business
- how tasks move between employees
- where delays happen
- where information gets lost
- what employees repeatedly do manually
- what software the company already uses
- what the owner cannot easily see or measure
- what causes operational mistakes
- what slows down growth

Then AcquisiFlow proposes a system that solves those problems.

The sales message should focus on business problems rather than technical terminology.

For example:

Bad:
"We build Next.js applications with AI and APIs."

Better:
"We'll build one system where your team can manage every customer, job, schedule, payment, and status."

AcquisiFlow should never claim that custom software is always the right solution.

If an existing SaaS product already solves the client's problem well, AcquisiFlow should be willing to recommend it.

Custom software should be recommended when:
- existing software does not fit the workflow
- the business is using too many disconnected tools
- important processes are still manual
- the company has unique operational requirements
- the business is growing beyond its current system
- the cost of inefficiency is becoming meaningful
